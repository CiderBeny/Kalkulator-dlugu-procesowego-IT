📐 Methodology & Sources

About This Tool

Process Debt Engine is a static single-page application (SPA) that estimates the financial cost of process debt in software delivery. It combines a strategic diagnostic questionnaire (Q1-Q12) with a multi-factor financial model to calculate annual OPEX waste, risk exposure, pipeline erosion, and NPV/IRR economics. No data is sent to any server — all inputs are processed locally in your browser. Your diagnostic state is encoded in the URL hash for easy sharing, and calibration data is stored only in your browser's localStorage. Exchange rates are fetched client-side from the public NBP API. This tool provides directional estimates only and should not replace professional financial analysis. All 'tool estimate' multipliers are user-configurable via the Advanced Parameters panel.

Source: Built as a static SPA with vanilla JavaScript, Chart.js, jsPDF, html2canvas, and SheetJS. Methodology based on BLS, OECD, DORA, COSO, SHRM, and Exepron research (see sections 1-16).

1. Annual OPEX Waste

Formula: (Manual hrs/yr + Chase hrs/yr) × Blended Rate × Team Size

Assumptions: 1,800 productive hours per engineer per year — derived from U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics American Time Use Survey (2024), which reports full-time employees average 8.1 hours on workdays, and OECD data (2024) calculating ~1,811 actual hours worked per US worker annually after accounting for paid leave, holidays, and sick time. The 1,800-hour baseline is user-adjustable via the Annual Productive Hours slider (Section 10).

Source: BLS American Time Use Survey 2024 (bls.gov); OECD Average Annual Hours Actually Worked 2024 (oecd.org).

2. Risk Exposure

Formula: Annual failures (Q5 × 4) × MTTR (Q11) × Downtime cost/hr × (Risk level / 5)

Assumptions: MTTR (Mean Time To Repair) defaults to 4 hours per incident — a conservative estimate aligned with DORA's "Medium" performer recovery time. Adjust this to match your organisation's actual incident resolution data.

Source: Standard actuarial risk exposure formula (probability × impact) consistent with COSO Enterprise Risk Management framework (2017) and NIST Special Publication 800-30 Rev. 1 — Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments.

3. Pipeline Margin Erosion

Formula: Opportunity margin × 0.25

0.25 multiplier (tool estimate): Derived from Reinertsen's Cost of Delay framework (Principles of Product Development Flow, 2009): delayed projects lose value at ~2%/month, or ~25%/year. The 0.25 multiplier reflects year-1 erosion — a conservative application of the CoD principle.

Source: Reinertsen, D.G. — Principles of Product Development Flow (2009). Exepron — "The True Cost of Delay" (2026). exepron.com.

5. Turnover Cost Model

Formula: Turnover rate × Team Size × Blended Rate (Q6) × 1,800 hrs

Assumptions: Uses your actual Team Size and Blended Rate (Q6) multiplied by 1,800 annual hours (aligned with ANNUAL_HOURS_PER_ENGINEER across the model). The $150/hr and 50-engineer defaults are no longer hardcoded — the model reads your diagnostic inputs in real time.

Source: SHRM Foundation — "Retaining Talent: A Guide to Analyzing and Managing Employee Turnover" (2025 update). SHRM 2025 Benchmarking Reports, shrm.org/benchmarking.

6. DORA Benchmark Thresholds

The four software delivery performance clusters (Elite, High, Medium, Low) correspond to the 2024 Accelerate State of DevOps Report published by Google Cloud's DORA team. Lead time thresholds: Elite <1 day, High 1 day–1 week, Medium 1 week–1 month, Low >1 month. Deployment frequency: Elite On-demand, High daily–weekly, Medium weekly–monthly, Low monthly–biannually. Note: DORA's four official metrics are Deployment Frequency, Lead Time for Changes, Change Failure Rate, and Failed Deployment Recovery Time. The manual-effort and human-error mappings in this tool are an adaptation, not official DORA categories.

Source: Google Cloud DORA — Accelerate State of DevOps Report 2024. dora.dev/research/2024/dora-report/

7. Exchange Rates

Live mid rates are fetched from Narodowy Bank Polski (NBP) Table A via api.nbp.pl on page load. Fallback rates if the API is unavailable: 1 USD = 0.87 EUR / 3.67 PLN / 0.75 GBP.

Source: Narodowy Bank Polski — Table A mid exchange rates. api.nbp.pl.

8. Estimated Annual Drag

Formula: OPEX Waste (incl. context-switching premium) + Risk Exposure + Pipeline Margin Erosion

Note: Pipeline Margin Erosion (cOppDirect) is a one-time estimate reflecting margin lost in the first year of delay. OPEX Waste includes a configurable context-switching premium (default 15%, range 0–30%). All other components recur annually.

Source: Economic model synthesised from BLS ATUS (2024), OECD Annual Hours (2024), Exepron "True Cost of Delay" (2026), COSO ERM (2017), and SHRM Retention Framework (2025).

9. NPV & IRR Economic Model

Formula: NPV = One-Time Costs + PVIFA × Annual Recurring | IRR = rate where Σ(CFₛ/(1+r)^(t/12)) = 0

Note: One-time costs = Pipeline Margin Erosion + CAPEX. Annual recurring = OPEX Waste (incl. configurable context-switching premium) + Risk. PVIFA discounts the recurring stream using a 9.3% WACC (Damodaran 2025, IT Infrastructure median) over a 5-year horizon. If tax rate > 0%, NPV includes a simplified straight-line 5yr depreciation tax shield. The tax shield affects NPV only — IRR and payback are computed on pre-tax cash flows. IRR assumes a 6-month implementation ramp (3mo zero savings, 3mo 50%) for realistic returns. Discounted Payback uses monthly DCF with the same 6-month implementation ramp (3mo zero savings, 3mo 50%) to find the break-even month. IRR (Internal Rate of Return) is computed via binary search over monthly cash flows — the rate at which NPV = 0. IRR > WACC indicates value creation. This aligns with standard capital budgeting (Brealey, Myers & Allen) and COSO ERM 2017 guidance on multi-period risk assessment.

Source: Brealey, R.A., Myers, S.C. & Allen, F. — Principles of Corporate Finance (14th ed., McGraw-Hill). COSO — Enterprise Risk Management — Integrating with Strategy and Performance (2017). WACC benchmark: Damodaran, A. — Cost of Capital by Sector (Jan 2025), IT Infrastructure median 9.3%.

10. Configurable Advanced Parameters

Thirteen parameters in the "Advanced Parameters" panel let you override default coefficients in real time: Pipeline Erosion Rate (0.0–1.0), WACC / Discount Rate (5–20%), NPV Time Horizon (3–10 years), Automation Lever Recovery (10–60%), Risk Lever Recovery (20–80%), Context-Switching Premium (0–30%), Corporate Tax Rate (0–50%, for the CAPEX depreciation tax shield), Annual Productive Hours (1,500–2,500, default 1,800), Innovation Runway Lever Recovery (10–80%), Management Efficiency Lever Recovery (5–40%), Retention & Burnout Lever Recovery (10–60%), and Scenario C overrides: automation level (50–100%) and CAPEX multiplier (10–30%). Changing any slider immediately recalculates all dependent metrics. Defaults are restored on page reload.

Source: User-configurable — overrides the Economic Model defaults (Section 9) in real time.

11. Cross-Variable Correlations

Four causal links between diagnostic inputs, each with configurable strength: (1) Higher Manual Effort (Q1) increases Human Errors (Q5) — manual steps introduce mistakes. (2) Higher Manual Effort (Q1) increases Management Overhead (Q7) — manual processes require more chase-time. (3) Better Documentation Standard (Q3) decreases Management Overhead (Q7) — clear docs reduce coordination. (4) Lower Documentation (Q3) increases Manual Effort (Q1) — knowledge gaps force rework. Default multiplier = 0.3, adjustable in range 0–1. Disabled by default — enables only when the user checks "Cross-Variable Correlations" in Advanced tab.

Source: Design assumption based on common DevOps causal chains. Not empirically validated — use with awareness.

12. Nonlinear Cost Model

Two non-linear adjustments applied when enabled: (1) Economies of scale — effective team size = actual team size ^ 0.9, meaning a 100-engineer team costs less than 10x a 10-engineer team. (2) Diminishing automation returns — the effective automation level = 1 - (1 - target)^1.2, so the first 30% of automation yields more savings than the last 30%. Disabled by default.

Source: Economies of scale: standard production function (Cobb-Douglas with a=0.9). Diminishing returns: common observation in process automation literature — initial quick wins taper off.

13. Monte Carlo Confidence Intervals

When enabled, runs 1,000 iterations of the full model with perturbed inputs. Each input (except riskLevel which is discrete 1-5) is sampled from a triangular distribution centred on the user's value with +/-15% spread. MTTR uses a log-normal distribution (Box-Muller transform) with +/-25% spread, reflecting the heavy-tailed nature of incident recovery times. Outputs are reported as median with 90% confidence interval [p5, p95]. A seeded PRNG (LCG, seed=42) ensures reproducibility across sessions. Disabled by default.

Source: Standard Monte Carlo method (Metropolis & Ulam 1949). Box-Muller transform for log-normal MTTR. 90% CI is a common regulatory reporting standard (Basel II/III, Solvency II).

14. Multi-Category Risk Model

When enabled, splits the single Risk Exposure metric into three sub-categories aggregated into total cRisk. (1) Operational Risk — the original failures x MTTR x cost formula. (2) Security Risk — operational risk scaled by manual effort ratio (more manual work correlates with higher breach surface) x 0.4. (3) Regulatory Risk — operational risk scaled by documentation gap (1 - docStandard/5) x 0.25, reflecting that poor documentation increases compliance exposure. The three categories sum to a higher total than the basic model alone. Disabled by default.

Source: Operational risk: COSO ERM (2017). Security risk scaling: NIST CSF 2.0 — manual processes increase attack surface. Regulatory risk: ISO 27001 clause 7.5 — undocumented processes fail audits.

15. Model Calibration

The Model Calibration panel lets you compare model predictions against your actual quarterly data. Each metric row shows: (1) Predicted value — the model's annual estimate divided by 4. (2) Your actual value — entered per quarter. (3) Variance — actual minus predicted. (4) Delta % — (actual - predicted) / predicted x 100. (5) Accuracy badge — green (<=15% error — Accurate), yellow (<=30% — Off), red (>30% — Poor). An overall Accuracy Score averages all metric delta %. Actual values are saved to localStorage and persist across sessions. Use "Reset Actuals" in the panel header to clear all entered data.

Source: Built-in calibration tool — user-provided actuals stored in localStorage.

16. Sensitivity Views (Worst / Base / Best Case)

The Sensitivity Views panel recalculates scenarios B (Targeted Investment) and C (Full Automation 80%) under three coordinated assumption sets while keeping the measured cost baseline (Q1, Q4, Q6, Q7, Q8, Q9) authoritative. Conservative (worst case): automation recovers less of the debt (autoLevel x 0.75, automation & risk recovery levers x 0.70), incidents are 15% more frequent and MTTR 25% longer, opportunity erodes 15% faster, CAPEX overruns by 20%, and the discount rate rises x 1.30 (to 12.1% at the default WACC). Aggressive (best case): the mirror image — autoLevel x 1.15, levers x 1.30, failures x 0.85, MTTR x 0.80, erosion x 0.85, CAPEX x 0.90, discount rate x 0.80. Base perturbs nothing and always equals the headline model. This complements the tornado chart (per-input x 20% swings) and Monte Carlo (full probability distributions) rather than replacing them.

Source: Scenario planning conventions (BASE case / WORST / BEST) as used in project finance sensitivity analysis; multipliers defined in PDE.SENSITIVITY_VIEWS (src/config.js). Recovery-dominant design avoids the paradox where scaling the cost baseline up inflates the savings an investment can claim.
